Chat with a book → RAG

Upload a book and ask questions about it. A small AI model runs in your browser. Before each answer, the page finds the 3 passages that best match your question and gives only those to the AI. This is called retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).

How it works

1. Cut the book into chunks

The book is split by paragraph into chunks of about 60–200 words. A small AI can't read a whole book at once. This model can read about 4,000 tokens, which is roughly 3,000 words.

2. Search the chunks

When you ask a question, every chunk gets a score with BM25, an improved version of TF-IDF. Rare words from your question, like names, count for more than common words. The top 3 chunks win.

3. Add them to a hidden prompt

A hidden instruction is sent before your question: "Answer ONLY using the following text snippets. If the answer is not in the text, say you do not know." The 3 chunks come after it.

4. Stream the answer

The model runs on your graphics card with WebGPU, in a background worker. It predicts one token at a time, and each token appears as soon as it is ready.

Why can it get things wrong?

If the search misses the right passage, the AI can't see it. Try the character's name or other exact words from the book. Small models also make mistakes sometimes, so check the sources.

What do I need?

A browser with WebGPU, such as a recent Chrome or Edge on a computer. The first download is large. After that, it works offline from your browser's cache.
